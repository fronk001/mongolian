# G — Verb booklet for practice videos

**Status: week 1 built 25 Oct 2026, waiting for Luna's check** (§7). Fred and Luna want to film
practice sessions: during the week Fred works through a booklet in GoodNotes,
then on camera Luna checks his homework and they act out a short scenario.
This note records what the booklet rests on, so it can be built without
redoing the research.

**Page convention.** As in F: `printed pN [PDF M]`. Beg = PDF − 1, MM = PDF − 12.
"300w" is Nomiin Ger's *The most common 300 words in Mongolian*.

---

## 1. The brief (Fred, 25 Oct 2026)

- 5 verbs a week, each in five forms: present continuous -ж/-ч байна, past
  -сан⁴, future -на⁴, habitual -даг⁴, negative -хгүй.
- Easy sentences for everyday use by a 26-year-old office worker.
- Near-memorisable: Fred learns them during the week and says them on video.
- Done in GoodNotes on the iPad; about 15 minutes a day.
- On camera: homework check, then a role-play scenario.

## 2. The five forms — where the rules are

| Form | Meaning | Rule | Beg | MM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -ж / -ч байна | is doing now | -ч after stems in в, г, р, except яв-, ир-; vowel inserted per harmony after a consonant (уулзаж, бодож); и after ж, ч, ш (уншиж, бичиж); ь → и | printed p35-36 [PDF 36-37] | printed p15-16 [PDF 27-28] |
| -сан⁴ | did | -сан/-сэн/-сон/-сөн by harmony | printed p65-66 [PDF 66-67] | printed p45-46 [PDF 57-58] |
| -на⁴ | will do (also "does") | -на/-нэ/-но/-нө by harmony | printed p84 [PDF 85] | printed p4-5 [PDF 16-17] |
| -даг⁴ | usually does | -даг/-дэг/-дог/-дөг by harmony | (байдаг only) printed p49-50 [PDF 50-51] | printed p34-35 [PDF 46-47] |
| -хгүй | won't do | stem + -хгүй, the negative of -на | printed p84 [PDF 85] | — |

**-хгүй is the negative of the future, "won't / not going to"** (Beg printed
p84 [PDF 85]: Би маргааш ажиллахгүй, "I will not work tomorrow"). The other
negatives are different endings: -аагүй⁴ "didn't" (Beg printed p65), -аагүй
байна "isn't doing" (Beg printed p35), -даггүй "doesn't usually" (Beg printed
p49-50, for байх). In speech -хгүй also serves as a general "I don't / I won't"
refusal. That nuance needs Luna. -хгүй байна means "should be but isn't" (Beg
printed p35) and is not the same form.

**The risk is the stem, not the ending.** Vowel harmony picks one of four
endings, but stems change too: fleeting vowels (амрах → амарсан, туслах →
тусалсан, эхлэх → эхэлнэ, амьдрах → амьдардаг), soft sign (ярих → ярьсан), and
long vowels (уух → уусан, уугаагүй with an inserted г, Beg printed p65).
ROADMAP §4 names this as the main risk of generating forms. The plan:
generate each form by rule, check it against the book text, and mark any
form no book prints, so that Luna sees those first.

## 3. Which verbs, in what order

**The list.** 300w items 238-284 [PDF 8-10] give 47 verbs as stems (асуу-,
бай-, цэвэрлэ-, …). On 25 Oct 2026 `words.json` had 23 of them.

**The frequency.** Krylov (2018) counts word forms in the General Corpus of
Modern Mongolian (GKMYa, about 1.16M words) and lists the 454 that occur
more than 255 times. Summed per verb over those forms (a lower bound,
since rarer forms are not listed): гэх 33274, байх 29206, болох 14597, ирэх 5553, явах 5417,
хэлэх 3086, гарах 2767, харах 2648, орох 2618, хийх 2102, мэдэх 1910, бодох
1471, барих 1426, өгөх 1413, суух 1319, ярих 1151, очих 958, үзэх 939, олох
685, чадах 532, авах 434. The corpus leans literary, so this orders the verbs
but does not decide them; office usefulness does that.

**Left out:** байх (it is already the auxiliary in -ж байна), гэх and болох (top
three, but mostly grammatical: quoting, "become", "may"; later),
дасгал хийх (folded into хийх), and өмсөж үзэх "try on" (shopping, not office life).
**Added**, for an office day: хүлээх, чадах, олох, очих, залгах, илгээх.
That makes 44 + 6 = 50 verbs, 10 weeks of 5. A * marks a verb not on 300w.

## 4. Why sentence ladders

Each verb gets one everyday sentence, said in all five forms, with only the
time word and the ending changing. "Ladder" is the working name in this note
only. The booklet itself says "the five sentences for each verb" and labels
every form with its grammar name: present continuous, past tense, future
tense, habitual, negative (future) (Fred, 25 Oct 2026). It never uses "now /
did / will". Fred memorises 5 sentences a week, not 25
unrelated ones, and the forms are learnt as a contrast.

- Formulaic sequences predict fluency: in François & Albakry (2021, *LLT*),
  formulaic-sequence use was a significant predictor of fluency (p = .015).
- Wray (2008), as summarised in an ACLL 2013 paper: a learner of Welsh
  memorised a week's material for a televised cooking demonstration, delivered
  it fluently, and recalled much of it nine months later. That is this
  project's loop: a week of material, then a filmed performance.
- The caveat from the same literature: memorising alone does not always make
  people speak better. The scenario and the homework check are there to make
  Fred *use* the sentences, not only recite them.

## 5. Content rules

- Original sentences only. Every Mongolian line goes through
  `tools/overlap.py`'s check (importable, as `review-sheet.py` does), because
  the videos may be shown publicly.
- Nouns: Fred's known words first. An office world needs new ones (кофе,
  хурал, ажлын газар …), up to about 5 a week, boxed on the verb page and
  glossed from the books where they appear.
- First names only (Фред, Луна), CONTENT.md rule 8. Nothing committed says
  more about Luna than "a native speaker".
- Luna signs off a week before Fred studies it: memorised mistakes are the
  failure this protects against.

**Her load per week, estimated:** 25 verb forms (single words, quick), 25
ladder sentences, about 10 dialogue lines and 5 questions. That is about 65
items, roughly 20-25 minutes. The exercises reuse the ladder sentences and
add no new Mongolian.

## 7. How a week is made (`tools/booklet.py`)

```
py tools/booklet.py --selftest      # the form rules reproduce 45 forms the books print
py tools/booklet.py check 1         # forms vs rules and books, sentences carry their form, copyright gate
py tools/booklet.py sheet 1         # Luna's check sheet (same look as review.html)
py tools/booklet.py apply 1 luna-booklet-01.json
py tools/booklet.py pdf 1           # Fred's booklet + Luna's answer key
```

- A week is `content/booklet/week-NN.json` (gitignored). The ten-week order
  is in `content/booklet/plan.json`. Each verb stores its stem **as 300w
  prints it** (бос-, ажилла-, тусал-). The spelling cannot tell
  ажилла- from уулз-, so the rule guesses only as a check.
- Exercises never add Mongolian. Tuesday to Friday's answers all point at
  the five sentences for each verb, so what Luna checks is all there is.
  Only the Friday questions and the dialogue are new lines, and she checks
  those too.
- Her yes is stored with the exact text (`signed`). An edited line goes back
  to unchecked. While anything is unchecked, every PDF page says "DRAFT:
  Luna has not checked this yet". A rejected word goes into `rejected` for
  a person to fix, because her note may correct the spelling or the
  meaning.
- The files go to `G:\My Drive\Learning\Languages\Mongolian\Booklet\`, which
  is Drive for Desktop's copy of the Drive folder, so they reach the iPad.
  `--out DIR` sends them elsewhere.
- Week 1 (25 Oct 2026): 87 items to check. 24 of the 25 forms are printed in
  a book or in Krylov's list; босож байна is not, and her sheet says so. The
  gate rejected two first drafts (a sentence printed verbatim in Beg, and a
  question sharing five words with one), which were rewritten.
